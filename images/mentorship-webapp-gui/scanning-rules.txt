Everything this week's work needs is inside this lab:

    the portal   http://portal.lab:5000      (the target)
    the scanner  http://dast:8888            (admin / student)

Those are yours.  Attack them as hard as you like.

Do NOT point Burp, the scanner, nmap, or anything else in this lab
at a website, an address, or a service that you do not have
written permission to test.

Scanning or attacking a computer you do not have permission to
touch is a crime.  In the United States that is the Computer
Fraud and Abuse Act, and most other countries have their own
version.  People are prosecuted and people go to prison for it.
It will not help you that you were learning, that you broke
nothing, that the system was wide open, or that you stopped when
you realized.  The offense is the access, not the damage.

Written permission means a signed engagement letter, a contract,
or a bug bounty program whose published scope names the target
you are testing.  "They probably would not mind" is not
permission.  A friend saying "sure, try it" about a system their
employer owns is not permission either.

One more thing specific to this lab.  It runs on your own machine
and uses your own internet connection, so there is nothing
between a mistyped address and a real company's servers.  A
scanner aimed one character wrong will happily go and find them.
Check the target before every scan.

If you want somewhere legal to practise outside this course, look
for a bug bounty program with a published scope, or stand up your
own copy of a deliberately vulnerable app.
